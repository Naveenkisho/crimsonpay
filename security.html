Security & Compliance

A detailed overview of wallet safety, transaction controls, infrastructure protection, monitoring, compliance boundaries, and user responsibilities.

Effective 1 August 2026Version 1.0Detailed policy
These pages explain platform rules and safeguards. They do not claim that CrimsonPay, Trust Wallet, or any named payment network has a partnership, licence, or endorsement unless a separate written announcement expressly confirms it.

1. Wallet safety model

The interface displays wallet, network, asset, amount, and destination before a user-authorised action. CrimsonPay will not request a recovery phrase or private key. Signing occurs through the selected wallet.

2. Transaction controls

  • Explicit review screens and sequential signing requests.
  • Configurable card and transaction limits, status tracking, and emergency administrative controls.
  • Public-address balance checks and on-chain confirmation references where supported.

3. Infrastructure safeguards

Controls include encrypted transport, restricted administration, secret separation, health monitoring, protected deployments, and logging of important operational actions. Sensitive credentials are not embedded in public pages.

4. Compliance boundaries

Availability may require KYC, AML, sanctions, fraud, source-of-funds, geographic, or eligibility checks by an authorised provider. CrimsonPay is not represented as an RBI-licensed bank, payment bank, PPI issuer, or payment-system operator.

5. Incident response

Potential incidents are triaged, contained, investigated, and documented. Affected services may be paused. Where law requires, users, providers, or authorities are notified without undue delay.

6. Vendor and integration risk

Wallets, chains, RPC providers, issuers, and payment networks operate independent systems. Their outages, policy changes, or vulnerabilities may affect service even when integrations are reviewed.

7. User responsibilities

Use authentic wallet apps, verify the domain, review every signature, update devices, and reject unexpected requests. Never send a recovery phrase, private key, OTP, or screen-sharing access to support.

8. Responsible disclosure

Avoid accessing or changing user data, stop testing if harm may occur, preserve evidence, and report suspected vulnerabilities through the official support channel.
